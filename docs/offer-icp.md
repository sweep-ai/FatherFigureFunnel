## ICP — "Comeback Dad" (client's avatar: "Mike, 43")

- Male, 35–50 (sweet spot 38–45), married/partnered, 1–3 kids ages ~3–16, US suburban.
- Business owner, manager, tradesman, sales pro, first responder. ~$80–180K income (targeting hypothesis, not a requirement).
- Beginner–intermediate; often formerly athletic. Wants to lose 15–30 lbs.
- Values: responsibility, faith (or openness to it), family, hard work, integrity.
- Buys when he trusts the coach and believes the plan fits his life.

### His reality
- Dependable at work — everybody leans on him. Home gets what's left: distracted, irritable, exhausted.
- Starts Monday with good intentions, loses momentum by Thursday. Knows what a squat is — knowledge isn't the problem.
- Remembers being confident and driven; wonders if getting older means accepting the decline.
- The real pain is the **gap** between the man he is and the man he knows he could be.

### Tangible moments (use these, not abstractions — per Kai's Nov 11 feedback)
- Pumping gas with his wife and kids in the car and realizing he couldn't protect them if something went down.
- Brushing his teeth with no shirt on and not recognizing the guy in the mirror.
- Clothes getting tighter; dodging photos with the kids.
- Saying "Monday I'm starting" for the fifth Monday in a row — and being the only one who knows he broke the promise.

### Internal dialogue (his words)
- "I used to be in pretty good shape. What happened?"
- "I know what to do. I just can't stay consistent."
- "I'm always taking care of everybody else."
- "I don't need to look like a bodybuilder. I just want to feel good again."
- "I want my kids to remember me as the dad who actually did things with them."
- "I can't keep saying I'll start next Monday."

**Messaging guardrail:** acknowledge the pain without shaming him or implying his body determines his worth as a father.
